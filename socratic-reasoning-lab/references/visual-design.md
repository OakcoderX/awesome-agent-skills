# Storyboard and visual-design adapter

Review whether the intended perception is likely to follow from the actual visible and audible design.

## Ground the medium

Identify whether the supplied artifact is a written shot list, boards, still images, an animatic, or rendered footage. Inspect pixels or playback when available and needed. A textual shot list supports claims about the proposed sequence, not proof of final visual legibility, timing, acting, or viewer response.

Keep story facts and country, language, culture, genre, style, and protected qualities supplied by the user. Do not automatically add Japanese symbols or conventional dramatic escalation.

## Choose medium-specific criteria

Select the criteria relevant to the decision:

- spatial orientation, screen direction, eyelines, and movement;
- attention, framing, scale, occlusion, and information timing;
- cut motivation, duration, sound-image relationship, and rhythm;
- emotional legibility versus productive ambiguity;
- production feasibility and continuity.

For interface or non-sequential visual work, replace shot-continuity criteria with hierarchy, contrast, navigation, affordance, accessibility, and the intended task. Do not force film grammar onto another medium.

## Compete and test

Distinguish missing visual information, poor attention guidance, an unmotivated cut, insufficient time, and deliberate ambiguity. More coverage, close-ups, music, explanatory dialogue, or backstory need not solve the actual problem.

Test a small change in one information channel. For example, compare alternative framing while holding performance and sound intent constant. Predict both the improved perception and the expressive quality at risk. Label viewer predictions as hypotheses until people or qualified reviewers examine a rendered result.

Do not invent images or shots that were never supplied. If only a shot list is available, give a feasible edit or test plan and disclose the medium limit. Preserve story canon and revision permission.
