# Business and operating-plan adapter

Review whether a plan's mechanism and arithmetic support the next business decision. Treat this as planning analysis, not personalized investment or legal advice.

## Separate the claims

Distinguish customer problem, willingness to pay, reachable distribution, delivery capacity, unit economics, cash timing, and durable advantage. A large market, a waitlist, compliments, or a letter of intent is not realized revenue.

Trace a proposed scale decision to its bottleneck. Useful competing explanations include weak demand, poor distribution, wrong segment, expensive delivery, and a measurement or attribution error. Keep interaction effects when one cause is insufficient.

## Check the economics

Use the provided inputs; show compact auditable calculations and units. Distinguish:

- revenue, gross margin, contribution after variable service costs, and profit after fixed costs;
- one-time acquisition cost, recurring revenue, churn or survival, and cash collection timing;
- average values from a small cohort and stable values at scale;
- founder or volunteer labor priced at zero and a repeatable paid operation.

If a cost, lifetime, refund rate, or cohort definition is missing, mark the conclusion conditional. Do not invent industry benchmarks or extrapolate lifetime value from one month of retention. Use scenarios or a break-even expression rather than a precise unsupported forecast.

## Find the cheapest decisive observation

Consider a limited paid pilot, a narrower segment, a delivery-cost measurement, or a message/channel test. State the difference between interview interest, deposit, completed purchase, retained use, and contribution. Preserve existing relationships and cash runway.

Propose a cap and acceptance criterion from the user's constraints or clearly label them for decision. Do not assume budget approval, contact prospects, promise delivery, change prices, or commit the business.

Recommend proceed, test narrowly, redesign, or defer with reasons and a reversal condition. A polished deck or a compelling narrative is not evidence that the model works.
