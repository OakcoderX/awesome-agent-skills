# Product and service adapter

Review whether the proposed behavior solves the intended user's problem under actual constraints.

## Establish the unit

Distinguish target users, purchasers, administrators, and people excluded by the current flow. Identify the journey step and observed behavior. Engagement, conversion, retention, task success, accessibility, reliability, and trust are different outcomes; use the user's decision criterion.

## Discriminate explanations

Useful competitors include an unmet need, discoverability or interaction friction, reliability failure, a mismatch in the recruited population, and invalid instrumentation. A conversion drop need not mean the feature is unwanted; praise in interviews need not mean repeated use.

- Inspect denominators, entry conditions, time windows, device or channel mix, and missing events.
- Separate people, sessions, and events. Do not compare cohorts with different exposure or follow-up as if randomized.
- Locate where users fail before proposing an entirely new journey.
- Look for successful and unsuccessful cases that challenge the same diagnosis.

## Choose a bounded test

Prefer the least disruptive test that distinguishes the live explanations: a focused usability observation, instrumentation check, prototype comparison, or properly scoped experiment. State what each would and would not establish. Do not call prototype preference proof of business impact.

When suggesting an A/B test, identify eligibility, assignment unit, primary outcome, guardrails, observation window, contamination risks, and how a meaningful decision threshold would be chosen. If traffic or evidence is insufficient, a smaller diagnostic observation may be more informative.

## Preserve and stop

Protect accessibility, privacy, reliability, existing expert workflows, and any explicit user constraints. Do not sacrifice these silently for a proxy metric. Stop at a recommendation or approved artifact change; deploying, messaging customers, collecting private data, or buying traffic requires the appropriate authorization.

Give one next decision and a result that would reverse it. Do not issue a generic feature backlog.
